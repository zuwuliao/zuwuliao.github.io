# From Knowledge to Creative - The way to work in AI era

I recently listened to Zhang Xiaojun's [podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E7qB_p1D0Xk&t=6116s) conversation with Professor Zeng Ming, former Chief Strategy Officer of Alibaba and a long-time researcher of corporate strategy.

What I found most useful was not any single prediction, but the framework he uses to understand AI as a new technological era. He connects AI with previous general-purpose technologies such as electricity, the Internet, mobile computing, and cloud infrastructure, then asks what happens to products, companies, strategy, and human work when intelligence itself becomes widely available.

I feel it very useful to me to understand where we are and where we will head to at AI era, I summarize his key points below. 

---

# Zeng Ming's View of the AI Era

## 1. AI is moving from infrastructure to applications

Zeng describes major technological revolutions as moving through three broad stages:

1. **Infrastructure formation** — the technology becomes mature, standardized, and broadly usable.
2. **Application explosion** — many products and business models emerge on top of the infrastructure.
3. **Native applications** — new products appear that could not have existed in the previous paradigm.

He believes AI is moving from the first stage into the second.

The first stage is foundation intelligence becoming infrastructure. Once intelligence can be measured, priced, and consumed in standardized units such as tokens, it starts to resemble compute, storage, bandwidth, or electricity.

The second stage is the rise of **Agents**.

At the begining of AI era, Human use a chatbot mainly answers questions, then human acts. With Agents, human defineds objectives, agents can plans, uses tools, acts, observes results, adjusts, then gets outcome that human wants.


This is why Zeng sees Agents as the new application layer of the AI era.

---

## 2. Foundation models become infrastructure

Zeng argues that model companies will increasingly resemble infrastructure providers.

As models improve:

- intelligence becomes cheaper;
- capabilities become more standardized;
- multiple providers become partially substitutable;
- application companies can call intelligence instead of building everything themselves.

This is similar to cloud computing. Most companies no longer build their own data centers or processors; they consume infrastructure as a service.

In this view, the model layer becomes something applications can rely on. Applications calls agents, and agents calls models, then comsume the result the model generates.

---

## 3. The important Agent is one that owns complex work

Zeng is skeptical of AI applications that automate only small tasks.

A feature such as, Generate an email, is easy for a stronger foundation model to absorb.

A much more defensible Agent would own a complete workflow like: Research customer, Decide next action, Contact customer, Observe response, Follow up, Evaluate outcome

The more an Agent participates in the full loop between decision and outcome, the more opportunities it has to improve.

That leads to one of the most important concepts in the interview: the **intelligence flywheel**.

---

## 4. The data flywheel upgrades into an intelligence flywheel

The Internet era produced powerful data flywheels:

```text
More users → More data → Better prediction → Better product → More users
```

The AI era adds another layer. When an Agent actually performs work, it generates real-world experience:

```test
Task → Reasoning → Action → Outcome → Feedback → Learning → Better capability
```

The system is no longer only collecting data. It is accumulating experience that can improve planning, memory, tool use, task decomposition, and judgment. That is what Zeng calls **intelligence compounding**. 

A simple way to express the difference is:

```text
Data flywheel:
More data → better prediction

Intelligence flywheel:
More experience → better capability
```

More importantly, I think his idea of the **intelligence flywheel** can be extended further: the most important competitive advantage in the AI era may come from the ability to generate new data, with recursive self-improving, create better capability, then generate new knowledge, and use that new knowledge to create greater intelligence.

---

## 5. Organizations move from positions to tasks and company may disappear

Another important Zeng thesis is that the traditional company was designed around fixed positions and hierarchy.

The old model of company consists of:

```text
Position → Employee → Manager → Department → Workflow
```

An AI-native organization may instead organize around tasks: 

```text
Objective, Tasks, Required capabilities, Humans + Agents, Outcome
```

This reduces the need for some layers of coordination and approval. 

Even more, company as a result of Industrial Revolution works well in Industrial, Eletricity, Internet era, but may not fit the AI era. In this new era, Agents are the workforce. Company as an orgnization to get the job done will disappear. New form of orgnization might be created and take over.

---

## 6. Strategy moves from planning to generation

Zeng also argues that traditional strategic planning becomes less effective in an environment where technology and competition change rapidly.

Instead of Predict future, Make planm, Execute plan, companies need a continuous strategy-generation system:

```text
Signals → Insights → Hypotheses → Experiments → Evidence → Decisions → New signals
```

The organization itself must continuously generate better strategic judgment.

---

## 7. Human value shifts from knowledge to creativity

Zeng argues that AI reduces the value of simply possessing existing knowledge.

If AI can absorb and reason over a large portion of human knowledge, then knowing more facts or procedures becomes less differentiating.

Human value shifts toward creativity, judgment, initiative, and the ability to create something new.

I strongly agree his this point. I will expand it in a littler bit.

# My Opinion — Agree, Disagree, and Extend

## 1. Agree: 

I have to say this is my first time hear someone analyze AI wave in such a system way. I agree most of Zeng's points such as AI becomes infrastructure, Agents become applications, analogy to the previous waves. 

Over time, intelligence becomes easier to consume, while more value moves toward systems that can use intelligence to accomplish real work. The current wave is showing AI infrastrucure is forming. OpenAI, Anthropic and others are building the leading foundation models and most of AI applications are built upon them. And current applications are more and more built with agentic workflow. We can simply say agents are AI infrastructure applications. Agentic behavior may simply become a normal property of software.

---

## 2. Disagree: 

**2.1 I do agree the company will change, but it will not disappear**

I agree that hierarchy will shrink and work will become more task-centric. We've seen the change has been started that many companies have reduced middle layer management and lots of workforces. Instead, they combine human and agents to delivery the outcomes.

However, I disagree company as orgnization will disappear. Some single person company will appeal. But most of organization  solve more problems than employee coordination.

It also provides:

- ownership;
- capital allocation;
- contracts;
- governance;
- liability;
- intellectual property;
- reputation;
- regulatory accountability;
- long-term commitment.

AI does not eliminate these functions.

Therefore, My view is the company survives, but its internal operating system changes. The future company may be much flatter and more dynamic, with humans and Agents collaborating around tasks, while the company remains the legal, economic, and strategic container.


**2.2 Model compainies and application companies can co-exist**

Another point I disagree, that is model companies and application companies have to be separate. Model companies become Infrastructure companies. That is true. However, I do not think becoming infrastructure prevents a model company from becoming an application company.

In fact, vertical integration can create a powerful loop:

```text
Better model → Better application → More real-world usage → More feedback → Better evaluation / training → Better model
```

The same can happen in the opposite direction: an application company may eventually build specialized models because its real-world experience gives it unique data and insight.

In the Internet era, we've seen this kind of companies already. For example, Google, Amzon and Microsoft, they are the main Internet infrastructure companies. But in the mean time, they create great applications as well. Google has Search, Google Map, YouTube; Amazon has online shopping; Microsoft has Office 365 and other workspace softwares. 

---

## 3. Expand

**3.1 Existing data is not enough for a moat**

For years, companies have described proprietary historical data as a moat. Once a company has run business in one area, it has accumulated a lot of domain specific data. But models have been trained with all Internet data and many domains data. If you want to use AI, you want the model been trained by your data. This makes static historical data is becoming easier for powerful models to understand and extract value from.

I would distinguish three types of data:

| Data type | Strategic value |
|---|---|
| Existing historical data | Useful, but increasingly easier for AI to interpret |
| Fresh operational data | More valuable because it reflects current reality |
| Newly generated data | Potentially the strongest moat because it expands what is known |

The real advantage is not merely owning yesterday's data.

It is having a system that continuously creates **new data that did not previously exist**.

How is new data created?

Through action and experimentation.

```text
Existing knowledge → New hypothesis → Action / experiment → NEW DATA → Evaluation → NEW KNOWLEDGE
```

This is where the Agent layer becomes strategically important: Agents can interact with the real world at scale and generate new experience.

---

**3.2 From data flywheel to intelligence flywheel to knowledge-creation flywheel**

I would extend Zeng's framework into three stages.

## Data flywheel

```text
Usage → Data → Prediction → Better product
```

The output is better prediction.

## Intelligence flywheel

```text
Tasks → Actions → Outcomes → Feedback → Learning → Better capability
```

The output is greater intelligence.

## Knowledge-creation flywheel

```text
Known knowledge → Human ambition + AI → New question → New hypothesis → Experiment → New data → New knowledge → Greater intelligence
```

The output is something that was not known before.

This, to me, is the deeper opportunity of the AI era.

The strongest organization may not be the one with the biggest historical dataset.

It may be the one that can **generate new knowledge faster than everyone else**.

---

# Human value shifts from understanding known knowledge to creating new knowledge

Finally, let's think about what human should do in the new AI era. I agree with Zeng that human value moves toward creativity, but I would frame the change even more strongly.

Historically, high-value knowledge work looked like this:

```text
Learn existing knowledge → Understand it deeply → Apply it correctly → Provide better answers
```

AI compresses much of that process.

The human role therefore moves toward the frontier:

```text
Curiosity → Ambition → Choose an important problem → Define a new question → Use AI to reason and explore → Form hypothesis → Experiment → Create new knowledge
```

The valuable human is increasingly not just the person who knows the answer.

It is the person who can ask:

**What important question has not yet been answered?**

and then use AI to help discover the answer. This is why I think **ambition** becomes especially important.

AI may know how to optimize an existing system. Humans still need to decide what new system is worth creating, what scientific problem deserves attention, what future should be pursued, and what risks are worth taking.

---

# Conclusion

Zeng Ming's framework helps explain several important transitions:

```text
Models → infrastructure
Applications → Agents
Positions → tasks
Hierarchy → networks
Planning → strategy generation
Knowledge → creativity
```

I agree with those broad directions.

Where I would extend the framework is around **data and knowledge creation**.

The AI era should not only make us better at understanding existing information or automating existing work.

The bigger opportunity is to create systems that continuously interact with reality, generate new data, discover new knowledge, and use that knowledge to become more intelligent.

The flywheel I find most interesting is therefore:

```text
Ambition → New question → Human + AI hypothesis → Action / experiment → New data → New knowledge → Greater intelligence → Greater ambition
```

If this is directionally correct, then the deepest competitive advantage in the AI era may not be access to a model or ownership of a historical dataset.

It may be:

> **the ability to generate new knowledge faster than others.**

And the changing role of humans may ultimately be just as important.

The old knowledge economy rewarded us for mastering what humanity already knew.

The AI era gives us the opportunity to spend more of our time exploring what humanity does **not** yet know — and to use AI as a partner in creating it.
